Getting started

Open any Confluence space and click Questions in the space sidebar, then Ask a question. Add a title, optional detail, and up to five tags.

The first question in a space creates a page called Questions. Every question becomes a child of it, so you can browse them in the normal page tree as well as in Trove.

Questions live inside a space, so they inherit that space’s permissions. If someone cannot read the space, they cannot see its questions — in Trove or anywhere else. There is no separate permission model to configure or to get wrong.

Finding answers

Search reads the answers, not just the titles. Search for a word that appears only inside a reply and the question still comes back, marked found in an answer.

Search: wireguard-config
How do I get on the VPN?  found in an answer
…settings live in the wireguard-config file on the bastion…
#vpn   #networking   3 answers · accepted

Results are ranked so the obvious one is first: an exact title match, then a title that starts with your words, then a title containing them, then question detail, then answer text.

Narrowing the list

  • Newest, Active, Votes, Unanswered, Accepted. Unanswered is the queue of unmet need.
  • Tags. Click a tag to filter, click again to clear.
  • Combined filters. Ask for questions tagged sso, by one person, with at least one answer — all at once.

Answering

Answers use the standard Confluence editor — headings, lists, code blocks, links and inline formatting. Because Confluence stores comments in that format natively, this is real Confluence formatting: it renders correctly in Confluence itself, in exports and in notification emails, whether or not Trove is installed.

  • Post an answer on any question.
  • Vote for answers that helped. One vote per person; clicking again takes it back.
  • Mark as accepted — anyone who can edit the page can do this, not only the person who asked. That matters when the original asker has left the company.

Keeping answers trustworthy

An accepted answer can be verified: a record of who confirmed it was still correct, and when.

After 180 days without verification, an answer is labelled not verified in N months. It is never hidden and never deleted. The label simply lets a reader tell the difference between “nobody has looked at this in a year” and “someone confirmed this last month”.

Avoiding duplicates

While you type a question title, Trove shows similar questions with a match percentage and whether they already have an accepted answer. Opening one of those is usually faster than waiting for a reply.

If you ask a question whose title already exists, Trove opens the existing one instead of failing.

People who may know

Each question shows people who have demonstrably answered on that topic, with how many answers they wrote and how many were accepted.

Expertise is calculated from real activity only. An accepted answer counts most, because it is the only signal where somebody other than the author judged the answer useful. Votes count less. Raw volume counts least. There are no points and no leaderboard.

Ask this person posts a comment mentioning them, so Confluence notifies them the way it normally does — through the channel they have already configured, not a second inbox to check. Each person can be asked once per question, and the comment says why they were picked.

Embedding questions in a page

Insert the Questions macro on any Confluence page to show that space’s recent questions with their accepted answers inline.

This is the point of it: on an onboarding page or a team handbook, the reader gets the answer where they already are, rather than a link telling them to go and find it.

Knowledge health

For space admins. Every number expands into the questions behind it — a count you cannot click through to is not actionable.

  • unanswered questions
  • questions with answers but nothing accepted
  • answers that have gone stale
  • clusters of likely duplicates
  • topics where nobody has an accepted answer yet
  • questions worth turning into articles

Convert to knowledge article

Turns a resolved question into a clean Confluence page with the problem, the accepted answer and its verification status. It links back to the discussion rather than copying it, so there is one place to correct if things change.

Rebuild from Confluence

Re-derives counts and tags from what actually exists. Because questions are ordinary pages, people delete them in Confluence like anything else — this tidies up afterwards. It never deletes anything itself.

AI assistance

Optional, and off by default. A space admin turns it on under Knowledge health.

  • Ask company knowledge — ask in plain language, get an answer drawn from that space’s questions.
  • Suggest an answer — draft a reply to an unanswered question from what has already been answered.

Every AI answer is labelled, cites the questions it used, and shows a confidence based on how much real evidence backed it. When the evidence is thin it says so plainly and offers to ask the community instead.

It only ever reads content you can already see. Context is fetched as you, so the model cannot surface anything your Confluence permissions would not show you. Models are hosted by Atlassian: no external API keys, and your content never leaves the Atlassian platform.

AI is off by default because model usage is billed to the app vendor, so switching it on is a deliberate choice rather than something that happens quietly.

Permissions and privacy

Trove stores no customer content outside Atlassian. There is no external server, no third-party data processor, and no analytics service. Every Confluence call is made as the signed-in user.

PermissionWhy it is needed
read / write pageQuestions are pages; so are the hub page and knowledge articles
delete pageDeleting your own question — Confluence decides who may
read / write commentAnswers and expert mentions are comments
read content-detailsFull-text search across answers
read spaceResolving which space a question belongs to
read userShowing people’s names instead of account ids

What you keep

Because questions are pages and answers are comments:

  • Confluence search indexes them
  • Confluence export includes them
  • The Confluence REST API reads them
  • Confluence page history is your audit trail
  • Space permissions apply automatically

If you ever uninstall Trove, every question and answer stays exactly where it is.

Limits

Worth knowing before you roll it out:

The list loads up to 500 questions per space; beyond that, search and tag filters find the rest. Question detail is plain text, though answers support full rich text. The macro shows a space’s recent questions; per-macro configuration is not yet available.
